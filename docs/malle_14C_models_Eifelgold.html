malle_14C_models_Eifelgold

Overview

Teneille’s full project repository is available at https://github.com/Teneille97/rocmics.

I modeled three pools (fPOM, oPOM and MAOM) with transfer from pool 1 -> 2 -> 3. The five parameters in each model are k1, k2, k3, a21 and a32, corresponding in the same order to decomposition rates of fPOM, oPOM and MAOM, transfer coefficient for fPOM to oPOM and transfer coefficient for oPOM to MAOM.

I used a fixed C input value and the same set of upper/ lower parameter limits for all the treatments, based on literature values, physical feasibility and trial and checking visual fits on preliminary runs.

We wanted to test whether the treatments are potentially enhancing SOC stabilization via aggregation or mineral association. This could be reflected by increased transfer coefficients, or greater mean ages of the pools, or longer transit time of the system.

Results

The parameters results for all the models were as follows.

Treatment (k_1) (k_2) (k_3) (a_{21}) (a_{32})
Control 0.271511 0.033169 0.005239 0.019364 0.027694
Bolsdorfer 0.334416 0.013386 0.025466 0.431038 0.005719
Huhnerberg 0.201884 0.016008 0.046393 0.023334 0.115156
Eifelgold 0.555058 0.017863 0.004482 0.386245 0.032701
Lime 0.388595 0.022714 0.017733 0.497479 0.000885

The coefficient for transfer of C from fPOM to oPOM was higher for Bolsdorfer and lime treatments relative to control, which shows more occlusion within aggregates in the Bolsdorfer and lime treatments. In contrast, transfer to MAOM was greater in Huhnerberger. Mean system transit times were longer in the basalt treatments, indicating greater SOC persistence.
